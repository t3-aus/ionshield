We want agreement on outcome before equipment selection.

The completed skid must deliver a homogeneous two-fluid aerosol of pre-blended 1.5% w/w PAA solution at Dv90 <10 µm into a 30-tonne grain pile void volume of 25,000 L, achieving 50 mg/L PAA vapour concentration throughout a 6-hour contact period. Verified performance criterion: 1.25 kg PAA delivered per batch, confirmed by the dose controller integrating FT-101 mass flow. Secondary criterion: nozzle exit concentration within ±5% of theoretical (0.953% w/w).

S2Stage 2 — Carrier-Water / Booster System (Q5–9)

Q5Do you recommend the Tecpro vertical multistage inverter skid as carrier-water source? Exact model and duty?
Identified as strongest Tecpro candidate for stable carrier delivery.

Yes, recommended — with a critical constraint. The Tecpro Vertical Multistage Skid with Inverter, 316SS wet end, is the preferred carrier-water source for this design. It provides pulseless flow, integral VFD/pressure-transducer PID, and compatible materials for water service. Required from Tecpro: exact model designation, pump curve, duty point efficiency, and NPSH required at 7 L/h and at minimum stable flow. The duty point (7 L/h at 4.5 barg) is almost certainly below the pump's minimum stable flow, meaning a recirculation line to WBT-201 is a non-negotiable part of the supply.

Q6What pressure stability during rapid downstream demand changes?
Zone switching changes demand abruptly; instability changes flow and dose.

Target: ±0.2 bar at the nozzle manifold header during zone switching (one zone closing or opening).

Zone switching air demand step: 116 NL/min (4 nozzles ON → OFF or OFF → ON) Receiver buffer calculation: Minimum receiver = Q_step × t_lag × P_nozzle / (P_storage − P_min) = (116/60) NL/s × 2 s × 3.0 / (7.5 − 2.8) = 2.5 L minimum Specified receiver: 150 L Buffer time at full zone load = 150 × (7.5−2.8) / (3.0 × 116/60) = 122 seconds Conclusion: 150 L receiver provides >2 minutes of buffer. Pressure instability during zone switching is effectively eliminated. FAT must demonstrate ±0.2 bar during a load-step test.

For carrier water: the Tecpro VFD PID must respond to zone-switching demand steps. FAT acceptance: PT-201 reading stays within ±0.2 bar of setpoint within 5 s of a zone switch.

Q7Minimum stable flow and maximum recommended flow?
Need the true usable turndown, not only nominal capacity.

Unknown — Gap G-03 (open). This is the most critical Tecpro-specific question for the carrier pump. 7.0 L/h is the design carrier flow. The Tecpro multistage is almost certainly rated at tens of L/min. Running at 7 L/h without a recirculation line risks cavitation, bearing damage, and pressure instability. Tecpro must state minimum stable flow for the selected model. If this cannot be demonstrated at or below 7 L/h, the recirculation line (back to WBT-201) must be sized and included in scope. Maximum flow is not a constraint here; turndown is the concern.

S3Stage 3 — Chemical Dosing and Mixing (Q10–15)

Q10How does Tecpro propose to meter active chemistry into the carrier stream?
Misting pump generates pressure; precision concentration requires dedicated metering.

This design's answer (for Tecpro to match or improve): PTFE-diaphragm metering pump (P-101), PVDF liquid end, 0–20 L/h at 8 barg. VFD-101 (0.37 kW) modulates speed and stroke. Range for this skid: 5–15 L/h (covers 25–50 mg/L). Turndown: 3:1 at constant accuracy. Target accuracy: ±2% of flow setpoint at 12.2 L/h. The 3 kW peristaltic pump originally in the BOM is rejected — it is approximately 1,000× oversized on hydraulic power (see Design Package §2 Correction 1). Tecpro may propose an alternative metering technology, but it must meet PAA material compatibility, ±2% accuracy, and 5–15 L/h range.

Q11How will chemical dose track changes in carrier-water flow?
Batch consistency depends on actual chemical mass delivery, not pump speed alone.

Active ratio control: carrier setpoint (FIC-201) = total nozzle liquid capacity target minus FT-101 real-time reading. As FIC-101 varies (e.g., on dose controller ramp), FIC-201 automatically adjusts to maintain constant total nozzle flow. This is not a fixed ratio — it is a live difference controller. The outer dose controller (DIC-001) integrates FT-101 × stock concentration over time and ends the run on delivered mass, not elapsed time. Multi-point FAT calibration: dose accuracy verified gravimetrically at 25%, 50%, and 100% of nominal stock flow setpoint.

Q13Where does chemical injection and static mixing occur?
Injection point and mixer determine whether atomiser receives a homogeneous formulation.

Injection via a 316L quill immediately downstream of FCV-201/FT-201 on the carrier line. PTFE check valve on the quill prevents carrier backflow. Immediately downstream: 8-element 316L static mixer, 25 mm bore, 300 mm length.

Static mixer residence volume: π × (0.025/2)² × 0.3 m = 0.000147 m³ = 0.147 L Residence time at 19.2 L/h total: 0.147 / (19.2/3600) = 27.6 seconds PAA degradation at 20°C in 28 s: <0.1% (kinetic models; verify by assay at FAT) Homogenisation: 8 elements provide >10 fold-and-split cycles; adequate for laminar Re = 143

AIT-201 sample point is downstream of MX-201, before zone valves. This ensures the sample represents the actual mixed stream going to nozzles.

Q14How will concentration be verified before atomisation?
Pressure and pump speed do not prove concentration.

AIT-201 on a sample slipstream drawn from the pipe between MX-201 and zone valves. A peristaltic sample pump draws flow continuously; sample returns to drain. The Q46/85 probe reads PAA in liquid phase. The mixed stream is ~9,530 mg/L (0.953% w/w) — confirm the analyser range covers this with ATI before specification (Gap G-01). A dilution stage (e.g., 10:1 inline dilution with clean water) may be required to bring the sample into the sensor's optimal range. FAT criterion: AIT-201 reading within ±10% of theoretical concentration.

Q15How will chemical stability through the dosing/atomisation train be demonstrated?
Chemistry preservation is a performance concern; measurement not assumption.

Pre/post-train assay at FAT Phase 3 (full formulation):

Sample 1 (inlet): draw 50 mL from TK-101 at batch start. Sample 2 (post-train): collect 50 mL from a nozzle manifold drain point or spray-catchment vessel during the batch. Both titrated by permanganate method (or iodometric — agree method with T3 before FAT). Acceptance: ≥95% PAA recovery (≤5% degradation through the train). Main degradation risk is NOT the 28 s mixer residence — it is TK-101 stock age. Specify maximum stock age on TK-101 (recommend <24 h from preparation).

S4Stage 4 — Atomisation: Critical Performance (Q16–23)

Q16Which Tecpro nozzle family and exact model?
Selection should be based on duty and measured performance.

IKEUCHI AKIMist 03C TN, sourced via Tecpro. 316SS body, titanium orifice insert, external-mix two-fluid. Flow at design point: 2.4 L/h liquid + 29 NL/min air at 3.0 barg each. New only — micro-orifice erosion risk prohibits second-hand sourcing. Tecpro to confirm this model is currently available and confirm the 3.0 bar duty curve (H03). If Tecpro recommends a different model or size, the flow balance must be re-verified against the 12.2 L/h stock constraint before acceptance.

Q17Internal-mix or external-mix, and why?
Mixing arrangement affects atomisation, air consumption and operating stability.

External-mix. Air and liquid meet outside the nozzle body at the nozzle tip. Advantages for this application: (1) no contamination of the air orifice by PAA deposits or crystallisation; (2) liquid orifice can be cleaned without disturbing the air side; (3) lower clog risk with an active oxidising chemistry. Disadvantage: requires slightly higher air/liquid ratio than internal-mix for equivalent Dv90. Internal-mix achieves finer droplets at lower flows but the increased clog risk with PAA in the internal mixing chamber is unacceptable for a remote-deployment system.

Q20How does droplet size respond to atomising-air pressure and air/liquid ratio?
Two-fluid architecture provides an additional control variable.

Air/liquid ratio is the primary droplet-size control variable. At 3.0 bar air and 2.4 L/h liquid: ALR = 12.1 NL/L. The PLC monitors PT-202 (air pressure) and FT-204 (zone air flow, to be added per Improvement #6). Increasing air pressure at constant liquid flow increases ALR → finer droplets but higher air consumption and compressor load. Recommended control strategy: hold air pressure constant at 3.0 barg; modulate liquid flow only (via FIC-101/FIC-201). Use air pressure only as a trim if droplet size drifts. Required from Tecpro: air/liquid operating map showing Dv90 contours vs. liquid flow and air pressure.

Q21What is the lowest stable liquid flow without dribble or unacceptable coalescence?
Establishes the minimum usable batch operating point.

Not established — must be tested at FAT. General guidance for external-mix two-fluid nozzles: stable atomisation typically requires a minimum liquid velocity through the liquid orifice sufficient to form a stable liquid sheet. For AKIMist, IKEUCHI publishes a minimum of approximately 0.5 L/h in water; PAA (lower surface tension ≈40 mN/m vs. 72 mN/m for water) may be easier to atomise at lower flows. FAT test: reduce liquid flow in 0.1 L/h steps from 2.4 L/h with laser diffraction measuring Dv90 at each step. Record the flow at which Dv90 exceeds 10 µm or intermittent spray begins.

Q22What nozzle-to-nozzle variation should we expect?
Manifold imbalance creates under- and over-treatment even when total pump flow looks correct.
Manifold hydraulic balance at 2.4 L/h per branch: Branch bore: 6 mm, length: 100 mm, Re = 143 (fully laminar) Branch pressure drop: 128 × 0.001 × 0.1 × (2.4/3600/1000) / (π × 0.006⁴) = 0.000021 bar Hydraulic pressure drop is negligible — nozzle-to-nozzle variation is driven by: (a) Nozzle orifice machining tolerance (±0.01 mm on 0.3 mm bore → ±6–7% CV on flow) (b) Mitigated by: cracking check valves on each branch Target: CV ≤ 5% nozzle-to-nozzle after check valves

FAT verification: individual nozzle gravimetric collection (one weighed catch tray per nozzle, 10-minute run). Calculate mean, SD, and CV across all 8 nozzles. Acceptance: CV ≤ 5%. Any nozzle more than 10% from mean to be replaced and re-tested before shipment.

Q23What air filtration, pressure regulation and flow control are required?
Air instability changes atomisation performance and batch consistency.

Air train (Skid 201): P-202 (oil-free, 7–8 bar storage) → 0.01 µm coalescing filter → refrigerant dryer (dew point ≤3°C) → 150 L receiver → PCV-202 (3.0 barg set, ±0.2 bar) → air PSV (5.0 barg, atmospheric discharge) → zone solenoids (XV-AA, XV-AB) → nozzle inlets. Filter differential pressure monitored by local gauge; change-out at 0.5 bar dP. Stability requirement: PT-202 within ±0.2 bar of 3.0 barg during any zone-switching event. The 150 L receiver delivers 122 s buffer — this guarantees stability during zone changes.

S5Stage 5 — 70-bar FOG Option (Q24–28)

Design position: The 70-bar hydraulic option is not recommended by this design. The questions below state the evidence required from Tecpro if they believe the hydraulic option can meet the outcome, and the formal decision gate that ends the evaluation.
Q24Does Tecpro believe the FOG 40/70 VAR 2 or APERTO can meet the required treatment performance?
Want engineering judgement supported by evidence, not assumption.

This design does not recommend it. Three technical objections: (1) 70-bar hydraulic atomisation through standard Tecpro nozzles produces Dv90 of approximately 15–35 µm — the requirement is Dv90 <10 µm. (2) Standard Tecpro FOG pump heads use brass, which is incompatible with PAA. 316L/Hastelloy heads would need to be specified and confirmed. (3) PAA chemical stability through a 70-bar triplex plunger (significant temperature and pressure spike) has not been demonstrated. If Tecpro believes the hydraulic option can work, they must address all three objections with measured data before this design changes its position.

Q25If proposed, what measured droplet distribution will a hydraulic nozzle achieve?
Nominal pressure is not proof of droplet performance.

Required from Tecpro if the option is proposed: laser diffraction (Malvern Spraytec or Sympatec HELOS) test at actual PAA formulation (1.5% w/w), actual flow and actual operating pressure. Acceptance band: Dv90 <10 µm. Test must be at the same liquid and air conditions as the intended skid configuration — not at 70 bar with water as a proxy. If Tecpro does not have a laser diffractometer, the test can be arranged through an independent spray laboratory; cost to be agreed before FAT.

Q26How does the hydraulic system behave during turndown?
A batch system may require variable chemical delivery; loss of atomisation quality at low flow is a process limitation.

Hydraulic atomisation is highly sensitive to turndown. For a fixed-orifice hydraulic nozzle: flow scales with √(pressure), so halving the flow requires only 25% of design pressure. For a nozzle designed for 70 bar, operation at 35 bar (half design pressure) dramatically degrades the spray cone and increases Dv90. At 25 mg/L (stock flow 6.1 L/h vs. 12.2 L/h), the hydraulic system would need to operate at roughly 17 bar — likely outside the fine-atomisation regime. Required: maximum-to-minimum turndown test covering the 25–50 mg/L range with Dv90 measurement at each point.

Q27What evidence does Tecpro have for chemical stability through the 70-bar pump?
The design assessment raises this concern; the correct answer is an assay, not an assumption.

No evidence currently available. Required assay protocol: (1) fill a recirculation loop with 1.5% PAA; (2) run the 70-bar triplex plunger at design pressure for 60 minutes of continuous recirculation; (3) draw 50 mL samples at t=0, t=15, t=30, t=60 min; (4) titrate each sample for PAA concentration. Acceptance: <5% PAA loss at t=60. Also monitor fluid temperature at pump discharge — PAA degradation is strongly temperature-dependent (doubles per 10°C rise). Record discharge temperature throughout the test.

Q28If hydraulic option fails the criteria, will Tecpro accept two-fluid architecture as design basis?
Establishes an objective decision gate before fabrication is locked in.
FORMAL DECISION GATE — 70-BAR HYDRAULIC OPTION

Two-fluid (AKIMist) architecture is confirmed as the design basis if either of the following is true:

(1) Laser diffraction testing of the proposed hydraulic nozzle at 1.5% PAA, actual flow and actual pressure does not achieve Dv90 <10 µm.

(2) Pre/post-pump PAA assay shows >5% PAA loss after 60 min recirculation at 70 bar.

This decision gate must be agreed in writing with Tecpro before fabrication is released. No informal deviation from the gate criteria will be accepted. If Tecpro cannot conduct the test before fabrication, two-fluid architecture is the default.

S6Stage 6 — Manifold, Zoning and Treatment Uniformity (Q29–33)

Q29How will the atomisation system be divided into treatment zones?
Treatment performance depends on spatial distribution, not total spray rate alone.

Two zones of 4 nozzles each. Zone A: 4 × AKIMist 03C TN (9.6 L/h liquid, 116 NL/min air). Zone B: 4 × AKIMist 03C TN (same). Each zone has an independent liquid solenoid valve (XV-ZA, XV-ZB, 24 VDC NC) and an independent air solenoid (XV-AA, XV-AB). Zones can operate independently (partial treatment, commissioning) or simultaneously (full treatment). Manifold architecture: 316L block with equal-length branches to each of 8 nozzles. The 2-zone split allows zone-by-zone commissioning and fault isolation — if Zone A has a blockage, Zone B can continue while Zone A is investigated.

Q30How will the manifold be hydraulically and pneumatically balanced?
Pressure drop can create systematic nozzle-to-nozzle variation.
Liquid manifold hydraulic balance: Branch conditions: 6 mm ID bore, 100 mm length, 2.4 L/h per branch Re per branch = ρ × v × d / µ = 1010 × 0.047 × 0.006 / 0.001 = 143 (laminar) Branch ΔP (Hagen-Poiseuille) = 128 × µ × L × Q / (π × d⁴) = 0.000021 bar (negligible) Conclusion: At these flows, hydraulic pressure drop is essentially zero. Nozzle-to-nozzle variation is driven by orifice manufacturing tolerance, not manifold dP. Balance valves are unnecessary. Symmetric branch geometry is sufficient. Air manifold: 12 mm header at 232 NL/min: velocity = 0.047 m/s (negligible dP) Each 6 mm branch at 29 NL/min × (3+1)/1 = 116 NL/min compressed → v ≈ 6 m/s Branch dP at 6 m/s in 6mm, 100mm length ≈ 0.01 bar (acceptable)

Balancing method: symmetric manifold geometry (equal branch lengths). Individual check valves on each liquid branch absorb the orifice tolerance variability. Individual pressure gauges on each branch for visual monitoring during FAT nozzle tests.

Q31How will spatial uniformity be verified at FAT?
Distribution problems discovered in the workshop, not on site.

Spray mapping protocol: Position a 4×2 collection grid of individual catch trays (one per nozzle position) at 500 mm from nozzle face. Run both zones simultaneously at design point (3.0 bar liquid, 3.0 bar air, 19.2 L/h total) for 10 minutes. Weigh each tray before and after. Calculate mean collection mass and CV across all 8 trays. Acceptance: each tray within ±5% of the mean mass (CV ≤ 5%). Any tray outside ±10% triggers investigation before shipment. Photograph the spray pattern (backlit against a dark background) to confirm spray cone geometry.

Q32How will partial nozzle blockage or failure be detected?
A blocked nozzle may leave total pump flow apparently normal while creating a local treatment deficit.

Two-level detection: (1) Zone level: one air flow meter per zone (FT-ZA, FT-ZB — addition from Design Improvement #6). A blocked nozzle in a 4-nozzle zone reduces zone air flow by 25%. Alarm threshold: zone air flow deviates >10% from setpoint for >30 s. Zone liquid flow is monitored via FT-201 (shared, but zone valve position provides partial isolation). (2) Local visual: individual pressure gauge on each liquid branch at the manifold. Blocked nozzle shows elevated branch pressure. Available to operator during FAT and at SAT. Full individual nozzle monitoring (one flow meter per nozzle) is not specified — it would add 8 instruments at a cost disproportionate to the benefit for an 8-nozzle pilot.

Q33Can individual zones be isolated for commissioning and maintenance?
Reduces commissioning time and improves fault finding.

Yes. XV-ZA and XV-ZB (liquid) and XV-AA and XV-AB (air) are independently operable from the PLC or via local manual override on the panel. Commissioning sequence: (1) Zone A liquid and air open; Zone B liquid closed, air open (air purge only). Verify Zone A nozzle performance. (2) Then Zone B. (3) Both zones for integrated test. Maintenance access: 600 mm service aisle in front of nozzle manifold face (per GA). Manual isolation handles (lockable) on each zone valve for maintenance isolation. Q34Which FAT instruments will Tecpro use and calibration status?
Measurement credibility matters as much as the result.

Required instrument set: (1) NIST-traceable calibrated deadweight tester for pressure gauges. (2) Calibrated weigh scale (±1 g, class M2) for gravimetric flow verification. (3) Laser diffractometer — Malvern Spraytec or Sympatec HELOS (third-party hire if Tecpro does not own one). (4) PAA titration kit — permanganate method, agreed with T3 pre-FAT. (5) Calibrated data logger for time-stamped trend capture (10 s intervals minimum). All calibration certificates must be submitted to T3 at least 5 working days before FAT starts. Any instrument not having a current calibration certificate is not acceptable for FAT measurement.

Q35Will droplet testing use the actual production nozzle/manifold configuration?
Testing a different setup may not represent the production skid.

Yes — mandatory. Droplet testing must use the 8 production nozzles in their production manifold configuration, with production zone valves, production air train (including PCV-202 and the 150 L receiver), and production liquid pump and flow meter. No test-rig substitutions. The laser diffraction probe will be positioned at 500 mm from nozzle face, which represents the near-field spray characterisation point. A separate far-field (2 m) measurement point is recommended as supplementary data.

Q36Will FAT use the intended formulation or a validated surrogate?
Water-only testing may not represent formulation-dependent behaviour.

Three-phase approach:

Phase 1 (water only): Proves carrier pressure, flow stability, and controls/interlocks. Safe, fast, full repeatability testing. Water surface tension (72 mN/m) will produce conservative (larger) droplets vs. PAA. Droplet size measured in Phase 1 is supplementary data only — not used for acceptance.

Phase 2 (0.5% w/w PAA): Proves dosing accuracy, mixing, and chemical recovery. Safer than full-strength stock. Droplet size measured — used to establish trend vs. Phase 1.

Phase 3 (1.5% w/w PAA — full formulation): Proves atomisation quality at production conditions. Droplet size measurement from this phase is the acceptance measurement. COSHH/safety controls required: PAA PPE, adequate ventilation, spill containment in FAT area.

Q37What numeric FAT pass/fail criteria?
Agreed before testing, not after the result is known.

See the pass/fail table above. All criteria must be agreed in writing with Tecpro and T3 before FAT Phase 1 begins. No criteria to be changed after FAT has started.

Q38How many repeat cycles?
One successful cycle demonstrates capability; repeated cycles demonstrate stability.

Minimum: 3 complete batch cycles at the 50 mg/L nominal setpoint (Phase 3, 1.5% PAA). Plus 1 cycle at 25 mg/L setpoint (Phase 3). Total minimum FAT cycles: 4. Statistical summary required: mean ± SD for delivered mass, peak pressure, flush endpoint time, and AIT-201 reading across the 3 nominal cycles.

Q39What is the procedure if FAT fails?
Need controlled engineering change and retest rather than informal deviation acceptance.

(1) PLC automatically captures and saves the last 30 min of trend data on test termination. (2) Tecpro raises a Non-Conformance Report (NCR) within 24 hours describing the failure, suspected root cause, and proposed corrective action. (3) T3 reviews and approves the NCR and root cause before any hardware change is made. (4) Engineering Change Notice (ECN) raised for any hardware modification. (5) After ECN implementation, the full affected FAT block is repeated — not just the failed test. (6) No partial acceptance, written deviations, or "accept subject to site resolution" without explicit written agreement from T3.

S9Stage 9 — Flush, Purge and Next-Batch Readiness (Q45–47)

Q45What automated flush and compressed-air purge sequence?
Residual chemistry affects next batch and contributes to deposits/blockages.

Step 1: P-101 off, XV-101 close (dose target reached). Step 2: Carrier continues at 7.0 L/h for up to 5 min → flushes H-1, quill, MX-201, and nozzle manifold. AIT-201 monitors concentration. Step 3: Flush ends when AIT-201 <50 mg/L AND FT-201 stable for 60 s, OR after 5 min timer — whichever comes first. Step 4: Carrier and zone liquid valves close. Step 5: Air blowdown — zone air solenoids (XV-AA, XV-AB) remain open at 3.0 barg for 60 s, clearing all liquid from nozzle orifices. Step 6: All zone solenoids close. Compressor continues to maintain receiver pressure. Batch record closed.

Q46How will the system prove residual chemistry is below agreed endpoint?
"Flush completed" is not the same as "system clean".

Primary: AIT-201 reading <50 mg/L in the mixed-stream sample loop. This corresponds to approximately 0.0005% w/w — below any meaningful treatment-level concentration. Endpoint must also be confirmed by FT-201 flow being stable (flush water, not residual PAA slug, flowing). If AIT-201 is out of service: draw 50 mL sample from the drain valve at the manifold, titrate with permanganate method; accept if <50 mg/L. FAT demonstration: run a normal batch stop and measure time from P-101 off to AIT-201 <50 mg/L. This becomes the design-basis flush duration and must be achieved within the 5 min timer.

Q52What three measurements does Tecpro regard as most important for proving commissioning?
Forces prioritisation of the variables that genuinely govern outcome.

From this design's perspective, the three measurements that must be proven at commissioning — because they are the minimal set that confirms the treatment chain is working end-to-end:

1. Delivered PAA mass per batch (FT-101 integrated, verified gravimetrically against TK-101 pre/post weight). This is the fundamental proof that the correct dose was delivered — regardless of what headspace concentration is subsequently achieved.

2. Droplet Dv90 at the installed configuration (laser diffraction at SAT, or equivalent surrogate evidence from FAT + zone-flow correlation). This confirms the aerosol is in the treatment-effective size range — without this, the PAA dose reaches the void air but may not remain suspended long enough for a 6-hour contact.

3. Flush endpoint PAA concentration (AIT-201 or titration). This confirms the system is clean before the next batch, which is the difference between a reliable batch process and a contaminated one that carries treatment chemistry across from one batch to the next.
